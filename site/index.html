Angiopoietin-1/Tie2 Activation as a Therapeutic Hypothesis for Hantavirus-Induced Vascular Permeability

Background: Severe hantavirus infection causes vascular leakage through endothelial dysfunction, inflammation, VEGF, angiopoietin imbalance, and glycocalyx loss. Main directions: The review examines HFRS/HPS permeability mechanisms, focusing on Ang‑1/Tie2 signaling and related pathways as a therapeutic hypothesis. Current state: Ang‑1 reduces hantavirus‑induced leak in vitro and angiopoietin dysregulation correlates with severity, but Tie2 evidence is mostly from other diseases. Gaps: Tie2 findings are inferential and no hantavirus‑specific animal or clinical studies exist for Ang‑1/Tie2 therapy. Future research: Conduct hantavirus‑specific animal studies and clinical trials to test Ang‑1/Tie2 activation.

背景
严重的汉坦病毒感染通过内皮功能障碍、炎症失调、VEGF介导的通透性增高、血管生成素失衡以及糖萼降解导致广泛的血管渗漏。(A1)
主要方向
本综述回顾了肾综合征出血热和汉坦病毒肺综合征中通透性机制,重点关注Ang‐1/Tie2信号、血管生成素失衡、VEGF、IL‐6转导信号以及糖萼损伤,并提出激活Ang‐1/Tie2作为治疗概念。(A2)
研究现状
体外汉坦病毒研究显示Ang‐1抑制内皮通透性,患者数据将血管生成素失调与疾病严重程度关联;大多数Tie2介导的保护证据来源于非汉坦病毒模型。(A3)
不足
Tie2激活数据主要基于败血症或其他疾病的推断,且尚无针对汉坦病毒的动物模型或临床试验评估Ang‐1/Tie2导向的治疗。(A4)
后续研究
需要开展专门的汉坦病毒动物实验和对照临床试验,以评估基于Ang‐1/Tie2的干预的安全性和有效性。(A5)
