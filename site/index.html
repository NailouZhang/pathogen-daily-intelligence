Fractional optimal control of hantavirus transmission between rodents and humans

Background: This paper studies hantavirus transmission between rodents and humans, a zoonotic disease with high mortality and expanding spread. Methods: A modified SIR–SIR model with Caputo fractional derivatives incorporates memory effects and three time‑dependent controls (rodent culling, sanitation, human treatment) and formulates an optimal control problem via Pontryagin’s maximum principle. Results: Numerical simulations using a fractional Adams–Bashforth–Moulton predictor–corrector scheme show that combined control yields the greatest reduction in infected humans and rodents, illustrating altered transmission trajectories due to memory dependence. Contribution: The study extends hantavirus modeling by integrating fractional dynamics and optimal control, offering insights into how memory effects influence intervention strategies and highlighting potential for improved outbreak management. Limitations: The model lacks empirical calibration and validation with surveillance data, and no real‑world sample size or location is reported, limiting immediate applicability to specific outbreaks.
