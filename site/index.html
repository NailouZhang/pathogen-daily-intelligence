Main directions: The review identified 33 studies (17,204 individuals, 2,221 confirmed cases, 135 secondary cases) showing person-to-person transmission as primary or co-occurring in 20 papers, with median incubation 20.8 days and serial interval 21.8 days; secondary attack rates were higher among sexual and close contacts, and ANDV RNA was detected in blood, saliva, respiratory secretions, urine, breast milk, and semen, with rare culture-confirmed replication-competent virus. Current state: Current evidence confirms biological plausibility of close-contact transmission and supports prolonged monitoring and risk-stratified follow-up of high-risk contacts, but recommendations such as post-discharge counselling remain provisional. Gaps: Uncertainties persist regarding the infectiousness of RNA-positive samples, the precise timing and magnitude of viral shedding, and the effectiveness of current public health interventions, highlighting methodological limitations and the need for standardized virological sampling. Future research: Prospective contact studies, standardized virological sampling, and genomic confirmation are needed to refine transmission dynamics and inform preparedness in endemic and non-endemic regions.
