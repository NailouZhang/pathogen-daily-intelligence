Can Hantavirus cruise into the next pandemic?

Background: The paper examines whether Andes virus (ANDV), a hantavirus capable of limited person‑to‑person transmission, could evolve into a pandemic threat, a concern highlighted by a recent cruise‑ship outbreak. Methods: An outbreak investigation was conducted after 13 ANDV cases were identified on the M/V Hondius (May–July 2026); public‑health actions included case isolation, quarantine, contact tracing, active surveillance, and genomic sequencing of viral S, M and L segments from cases. Results: Thirteen cases (3 deaths) were reported; viral genomes showed identical S and M segments and only two synonymous SNPs in the L segment, with ~98.7% nucleotide identity to historic Argentine strains, and incubation periods of 9–45 days, indicating limited transmission and no amino‑acid changes linked to increased spread. Background: Mammalian hantaviruses cause severe disease, but most entry mechanisms are unknown. Methods: rVSVs bearing Gn/Gc from seven diverse hantaviruses were used to infect primary human endothelial cells, including PCDH1‑knockout cells.
