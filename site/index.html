Hantavirus RNA Detection in Small Mammals and Spatial Comparison with Reported Human HFRS Cases in West Kazakhstan Region, 2025

Background: HFRS is a zoonotic disease linked to orthohantaviruses in rodents. Methods: A cross‑sectional study in 2025 captured 343 small mammals across four districts, testing lung tissues via pooled and individual RT‑PCR (137 units) and estimating infection rates with MIR and a likelihood model. Results: Hantavirus RNA was found in 47 units; descriptive MIR 13.7% and model‑based infection probability 18.0% (95% CI 13.5–23.1%); positives occurred in three vole species, with district MIR 11.6–19.0%; rodent sites overlapped human HFRS settlements, but spatial coincidence does not prove transmission. Contribution: Provides rodent surveillance and spatial linkage to human cases, aiding risk assessment in West Kazakhstan. Limitations: Based on abstract‑only data; spatial overlap does not establish causation.

Background: Mammalian hantaviruses cause severe human disease, yet their cellular entry mechanisms are poorly defined and many newly discovered viruses are known only from sequence data. Methods: Replication‑competent rVSVs bearing Gn/Gc from seven hantaviruses were generated and used to infect primary human endothelial cells; PCDH1‑deficient cells, soluble PCDH1, a PCDH1‑targeting antibody, and authentic Tula virus were employed to probe receptor usage; a broadly reactive anti‑Gn/Gc monoclonal antibody was tested for neutralization.
