Increasing spatial scale of analysis weakens and can mask the dilution effect for hantaviruses.

Background: The dilution effect—whether higher biodiversity reduces zoonotic pathogen transmission—has major implications for conservation and public health but remains debated. Methods: The authors tested how increasing spatial analysis scale influences the relationship between hantavirus infection prevalence and rodent species richness using a global dataset across multiple regions. Results: Species richness was negatively associated with infection prevalence at all scales, but the dilution effect weakened with greater spatial aggregation and could become non‑significant at coarse resolutions. Contribution: This suggests the dilution effect is a general feature of hantavirus‑rodent systems, yet its detection is highly scale‑dependent, highlighting the need for scale‑explicit approaches. Limitations: Not reported in the supplied evidence.

背景: Hantaviruses cause >200,000 annual cases of hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome and hantavirus cardiopulmonary syndrome, yet no specific antiviral therapy is approved. 方法: A three‑stage computational framework was built; transcriptomic analysis of Hantaan virus‑infected human umbilical vein endothelial cells identified 203 differentially expressed genes dominated by interferon‑stimulated genes; a STRING protein–protein interaction network (176 nodes, 3,210 edges) revealed 15 hub genes (e.g., ISG15, IRF1, CXCL10, STAT1, DDX58); Open Targets identified six druggable hubs and 12 repurposing candidates; an ordinary differential equation model of type I interferon signalling evaluated eight drug perturbations. 结果: Early IFN‑β treatment achieved the fastest viral clearance (3.5 days) with reduced CXCL10 immunopathology; early JAK inhibition delayed clearance (4.9 days) and increased CXCL10; combining IFN‑β with an anti‑CXCL10 monoclonal antibody produced the best balance between antiviral efficacy and immunopathology control, though the antiviral gain derives entirely from the interferon component. 贡献: The study offers a mechanistically grounded, evidence‑ranked framework to prioritize candidate therapies and phase‑specific combinations for preclinical hantavirus research. 局限: Not reported in the supplied evidence.

DNA and mRNA Vaccines Against Hantaviruses: Antigen Design, Delivery Strategies and Translational Progress

Background: Hantaviruses cause HFRS and HCPS, with no specific antivirals and limited licensed vaccines. Main directions: The review focuses on DNA and mRNA vaccine design, delivery, immunogenicity, safety, and translational progress; DNA candidates show immunogenicity and acceptable safety, while mRNA shows robust preclinical responses. Current state: DNA vaccine candidates have demonstrated immunogenicity and safety in preclinical and early clinical studies, whereas mRNA vaccines have elicited strong humoral and cellular responses in preclinical models but lack clinical evaluation; cross‑species protection is limited by antigenic diversity. Gaps: mRNA vaccines have not yet entered clinical trials, and cross‑species protection remains limited due to antigenic diversity. Future research: Further development of multivalent nucleic‑acid vaccines with standardized evaluation and clinical efficacy studies is warranted.

Andes Virus: From Transmission Dynamics to Pandemic Potential.

Background: ANDV has re‑emerged with a multi‑country cruise‑ship outbreak in April 2026, causing 13 cases and three deaths (A1). Main directions: The review surveyed PubMed, Google Scholar and health‑agency reports to summarise epidemiology, transmission, pathogenesis and clinical management (A2). Current state: ANDV is a zoonotic orthohantavirus endemic to southern Chile and Argentina, carried by Oligoryzomys longicaudatus, causes severe disease via endothelial dysfunction and vascular leakage, and uniquely spreads human‑to‑human through respiratory and salivary routes (A3,A5,A6).
