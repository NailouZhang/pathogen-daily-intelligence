A historical cross-border Andes virus lineage reveals the origin of a cruise ship hantavirus pulmonary syndrome outbreak

Background: Andes virus caused a multi‑country HPS outbreak on a cruise ship in 2026. Methods: Complete S, M, and L genome segments from Chilean ANDV and outbreak‑associated sequences were analyzed. Results: Phylogenies placed the outbreak virus in Clade III, closest to a 2012 Los Ríos isolate; Clade V from central Chile was unrelated, lowering spillover likelihood in Maule; origin traced to the Hua Hum Pass corridor linking Argentina and Chile. Contribution: The work narrows the outbreak’s geographic source, aiding surveillance and public‑health response. Limitations: Findings rely on limited genomic data and rodent sampling; full methodological details are not provided.

Bile acid signaling at the gut–vascular interface: a novel modulator of hantavirus endothelial barrier dysfunction

Background: Hantavirus infection triggers life‑threatening hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome and cardiopulmonary syndrome driven by endothelial barrier breakdown; clinical severity varies with undefined host regulators and no endothelial‑protective treatments exist, while recent data link hantaviruses to gut microbiome remodeling and bile acid (BA) receptors FXR/TGR5 that inhibit NF‑κB‑mediated endothelial inflammation. Methods: The study re‑analyzed three public GEO datasets (GSE245916, GSE7271, GSE270172) using standardized RNA‑seq/microarray pipelines and incorporated metagenomic reports confirming hantavirus reshapes gut/lung microbiota in rodent reservoirs. Results: SEOV‑infected human/rat lung endothelial cells showed VCAM1 log2FC +1.17 (P = 0.023) and rat ICAM1 log2FC +0.32 (padj = 0.016) with unchanged FXR; Nfkb1 was suppressed at all timepoints (P < 0.05) and Slc10a2 downregulated at day 15 (P = 0.028); PUUV 3D vessel chips displayed IL6 log2FC +1.22 (P = 3.1×10⁻⁸) and ABCC3 log2FC −1.44 (P = 7.4×10⁻¹²); TGR5 was undetectable in endothelial cells; HTNV upregulated CH25H, blocking HMGCR‑dependent cholesterol synthesis and depleting BA precursors. Contribution: The authors propose a unified model in which hantavirus‑induced gut dysbiosis and virus‑impaired cholesterol metabolism deplete circulating FXR/TGR5 agonistic BAs, relieving constitutive inhibition of endothelial NF‑κB and monocyte NLRP3 inflammasomes, thereby exacerbating capillary leakage; this axis represents a repurposable therapeutic target. Limitations: Direct causal evidence linking BA signaling to viral vascular damage is absent, and the study relies solely on re‑analysis of existing datasets without new experimental validation.

Maintenance of Hokkaido virus, a genotype of Orthohantavirus puumalaense, in the rodent host Myodes rufocanus bedfordiae under natural conditions.

Background: Orthohantaviruses have preferred natural rodent hosts, but the mechanisms of their maintenance and transmission in wild host populations remain unclear. Methods: Field surveys were conducted from 2022 to 2025 in a forest in Tobetsu, Hokkaido, Japan, capturing 199 gray red‑backed voles (Myodes rufocanus bedfordiae) and testing them for Hokkaido virus infection. Results: Twenty‑three voles were HOKV‑positive; five were RNA‑positive but IgG‑negative (acute infection) and 18 were RNA‑positive with high antibody titers (persistent infection). High viral RNA and antigens were consistently detected in lungs, kidneys, and spleen, and infectious virus was recovered from oral swabs (8/8), urine (3/6), and feces (4/6). Contribution: The study shows HOKV can persist at high loads in host organs and be shed via saliva, urine, and feces, especially saliva, expanding understanding of orthohantavirus ecology, persistence, and potential spillover risk. Limitations: Not reported in the supplied evidence.
