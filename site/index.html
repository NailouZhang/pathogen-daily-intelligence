Molecular Surveillance and Evolutionary Characterization of Orthohantavirus jejuense in the Republic of Korea, 2019–2025

Background: Jeju virus (JJUV), a shrew‑borne Orthohantavirus jejuense, has been detected in Crocidura shantungensis in the Republic of Korea, but its distribution and evolution are incompletely defined. Methods: Shrews (n=56) collected from 20 sites (2019‑2025) were screened for JJUV RNA; positives underwent next‑generation and Sanger sequencing with phylogenetic, evolutionary, selection‑pressure and in silico B‑cell epitope analyses. Results: JJUV RNA was found in 7/56 shrews (12.5%) from four locations; coding‑complete genomes were obtained for three variants (two from Gwangju). Phylogeny revealed geographic clustering of variants from the northern mainland, Gwangju, Jeju and Ulleung Islands. Evolutionary rates were 3.53‑13.49 × 10⁻⁴ substitutions/site/year, with low dN/dS ratios indicating purifying selection. Six conserved linear B‑cell epitopes were predicted, the Y165‑K175 nucleocapsid epitope showing strongest structural support. Contribution: The study expands known geographic and genomic diversity of O. jejuense and offers a framework for ongoing surveillance and future serological assessment of zoonotic risk. Limitations: Findings are based solely on abstract data; detailed methods and full results are not reported.

Background: Large language models are increasingly used for health information, but their reliability for outbreak communication is uncertain, especially during the 2026 Andes virus outbreak. Background: Hantaviruses are emerging zoonotic viruses that infect humans with high fatality rates.
