arenavirus et hantavirus chez les petits mammiferes sauvages terrestres de madagascar identifies par rt-pcr

Background: Arenaviruses and hantaviruses cause severe human disease and are linked to rodent/insectivore reservoirs; they had not been detected in Madagascar before this work (F1-F4). Methods: A prospective study sampled 586 small wild mammals (17 species) in Danorana forest, Danjozorobe district (Oct 2008‑Mar 2009), testing liver and spleen by RT‑PCR and sequencing (F5-F11). Results: No arenavirus was found (0%); hantavirus RNA was present in 35.9% of animals, revealing two putative new hantavirus species associated with Rattus rattus and Oryzorictes hova (F9-F15). Contribution: First documentation of hantavirus circulation in Madagascar and identification of two novel lineages, highlighting viral diversity and host range (F13-F15). Limitations: Findings are based on partial sequences needing full‑genome confirmation and are restricted to one forest and sampling period (F16).
