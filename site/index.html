Ho Wang Lee’s Research of Epidemic Hemorrhagic Fever and Discovery of Hantaan Virus

Background: Ho Wang Lee investigated Epidemic Hemorrhagic Fever (EHF) to identify its causative agent, a problem critical for disease control. Methods: From 1970‑1973 he used whole wild rats without success; 1974‑1976 he examined lung tissue of wild rats and applied the Fluorescent Antibody Technique; 1977‑1980 he performed physicochemical analyses of the identified antigen. Results: He identified the Korea Antigen as the EHF agent, clarified its physicochemical properties, and in 1980 the antigen was officially registered as Hantaan virus. Contribution: The work established Hantaan virus as the etiologic agent of EHF, advancing virology, surveillance, and public‑health response. Limitations: The article provides a historical narrative without primary experimental data; only abstract and partial text are available, and no explicit study limitations are reported.

Assessing the performance of time series prediction models for hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome across different high-risk areas in Shaanxi Province, China.

Background: Hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome (HFRS) is a climate‑sensitive disease, and current forecasting models often ignore prefecture‑level variations and environmental drivers. Methods: Monthly HFRS case counts and 14 environmental factors were collected from 2005‑2023 in four Shaanxi prefecture‑level cities (Xi'an, Baoji, Xianyang, Weinan). Stepwise screening identified core drivers, and four time‑series models—univariate SARIMA, LSTM, and their multivariate counterparts SARIMAX and LSTMX—were built and evaluated via rolling‑origin validation across 2021‑2023. Results: In Xi'an and Baoji, multivariate LSTMX achieved R² of 0.976 and 0.950 with RMSE reductions to 17.782 and 2.805, whereas in Xianyang and Weinan, univariate SARIMA performed better and adding covariates did not improve accuracy; sensitivity analysis showed the screened covariate set avoided severe loss of accuracy. Contribution: The study shows that no single configuration is uniformly optimal; covariate‑enhanced LSTMX suits some regions while univariate models suffice for others, underscoring the need for region‑specific model selection. Limitations: Pairwise differences were not statistically significant after Holm correction, rankings changed on incidence‑rate scale, and the analysis is limited to an abstract, precluding detailed assessment.

Background: Hantaviruses cause HFRS and HPS with high mortality and no approved antivirals.
