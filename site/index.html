Epidemiology and economic burden of Puumala virus infection in Styria, Austria, 2003 to 2024

Background: Hantavirus infections cause hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome and may burden healthcare systems, yet economic data are scarce. Methods: A retrospective cost‑of‑illness study examined 1,049 serologically confirmed Puumala virus hospitalizations across 20 public hospitals in Styria, Austria (Sept 2003–Mar 2024), estimating costs via the Austrian LKF DRG system (2024 €) and identifying admission cost predictors with multivariable γ‑GLM and multiple imputation; length‑of‑stay trends were analyzed by weighted segmented linear regression. Median length of stay fell ~50% over the period. Contribution: The study quantifies the episodic economic impact of PUUV hospitalizations and highlights predictors of cost, informing regional preparedness. Hantavirus: A Dangerous Scientific Cruise

Background: An outbreak of hantavirus on a cruise ship returning from Ushuaia raised concerns about zoonotic spread in confined travel settings. Methods: Thirteen cases among 150 multinational passengers were diagnosed by serology or PCR; the ship docked in the Canary Islands under safety protocols and WHO sampled >150 rodents, all negative.
